## 0. Constraints and the filter they create

Restated so every recommendation below can be tested against them:

- 10 hours per week, stable. Assumed to be mostly outside normal business hours.
- Long-term need: $90K–$120K per year. Near-term purpose: learn to create, run, and grow a business, and learn AI skills.
- Strengths: 30 years of B2B and phone-based work, tech support, college recruiting. Enjoys B2B relationships. No-code, building with Claude.
- The current client is unpaid. Finchby has proven capability, not demand.
- Day-job domain is background experience only, not a direction.

**The filter.** A service earns a place in Finchby only if it passes most of these:

1. The buyer already spends money in this category. No evangelizing a new budget line.
2. Delivery is mostly asynchronous, with calls that can be scheduled.
3. Claude can carry at least half the delivery hours.
4. It recurs, or the same buyer rebuys it, so sales effort is not repeated every month.
5. It plays to research, relationships, and the phone, not design or technical craft.
6. Effective rate of at least $100 per hour now, with a path to $150 or more as delivery gets templated.

**The arithmetic behind the filter.** At 10 hours per week with about half of it billable, Finchby has roughly 250 billable hours a year. At $100 per hour that is $25K. The 10-hour phase therefore cannot reach $90K. Its job is to prove pricing, build repeatable delivery, and push the effective rate up so that going full-time later clears the target.

### 1G. Where the money and the gaps overlap

Three categories are high-pain, already-budgeted or clearly valuable, and nearly unserved for a shop like Victory, and you fit all three:

1. **Trade-partner pipeline and follow-up** (sales operations). Highest leverage on revenue, no dedicated sellers, and your proven skill.
2. **Hiring** (flat-fee recruiting). The trade's loudest pain, expensive incumbents, your past skill, and evening-compatible.
3. **Software implementation and AI setup** (operations and technology). Vendors do not hand-hold; you are learning these tools anyway.

Two bundle components make retainers stickier without much extra time: **reputation management** and the **remnant-to-cash program**.

Everything else on the map (ads, SEO, web, bookkeeping, IT, compliance) is either crowded, daytime-bound, or outside your craft. Refer those out and earn goodwill, not margin.

## 2. An honest read on the Victory engagement

The unpaid work proved you can do the research. It did not prove anyone will pay for it, and that is the only question that matters for profitability. The next step is a priced proposal, delivered by phone, with two paid options rather than a yes-or-no:

- **Option A:** Trade-Partner Pipeline retainer. Work the 1,000-contact list on their behalf: a monthly partner newsletter, a follow-up cadence for open quotes, ten partner touchpoints per month, and a one-page monthly brief.
- **Option B:** One flat-fee hire, for whichever role is open now (installer, CNC operator, or office).

A "no" to both is still useful. It tells you whether the problem is price, trust, or perceived value, and that is the lesson the business needs most right now. Whatever happens, keep Victory exclusive among Austin fabricators and sell to adjacent trades.

### 3A. How these combine into one company

The cores are not random. They are two sides of the same work: research, outreach, relationship, follow-up. And they answer the two problems every owner of a 5–50 person trade or B2B business names first:

> **"I need more good jobs."** and **"I can't find good people."**

That gives Finchby a thesis that is broad enough to outgrow countertops and specific enough to sell:

**Finchby finds customers and finds people for small trade and B2B companies, and sets up the tools (CRM and Claude) so the office can keep it going.**

Two product lines, one method, one buyer. The generic name holds it comfortably.

### 3B. Should Finchby move to a completely different category?

No. Not to a different category of work. B2B services for small trade and B2B companies is the right category for your strengths, your available hours, and your interest in B2B relationships. The change that matters is **widening from "contact research" to "customers and people,"** and **narrowing the buyer** to owner-operated companies with 5–50 employees, starting with the trades around Austin because you already have a story there. Revisit this in twelve months with real revenue data, not before.

## 5. Profitability path

Gross revenue, before tools and taxes. Hours are total weekly hours including sales and admin.

| Stage | When | What is true | Mix | Monthly gross | Hours per week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0: First dollar | Months 0–3 | Victory gets a priced proposal; one or two pilots sold at a discount; delivery skills written down | 1 retainer or 1 hire | $500–$2,000 total | 10 |
| 1: Proof | Months 3–12 | Three paying clients, two case studies, a price sheet you no longer apologize for | 2 retainers, 1 hire per quarter, 1 setup per quarter | $2,000–$3,500 | 10 |
| 2: Templated | Year 2–3 | Skills and SOPs cut delivery hours in half; referrals start | 3 retainers, 1 hire per month, 2–3 bundle clients | $5,000–$8,000 | 10 (this is the ceiling at 10 hours) |
| 3: Full-time trigger | When Stage 2 holds for 3 consecutive months with 5+ warm conversations in the pipeline | Hours expand to 35–40 | 6–8 retainers, 2–3 hires per month, setups, bundles | $12,000–$18,000 | 35–40 |

Stage 3 clears $90K–$120K net after tools, taxes, and a modest contractor budget. Two things govern whether you reach it, and neither is delivery quality: **conversion** (how many conversations become paid work) and **effective rate** (how far Claude templating pushes hours down). Track both monthly from day one.

## 6. Learning plan tied to the products

### Business skills, in order

1. **Pricing and proposals.** Value-based, fixed-scope, two-option proposals. Practice on Victory this month. Read Jonathan Stark's *Hourly Billing Is Nuts* and Blair Enns's *Pricing Creativity*; both are short.
2. **Discovery calls.** A written 20-minute script: their volume, their partner sources, their open roles, their current tools, what a good month looks like. Your phone background makes this your fastest win.
3. **Scope and change control.** One-page SOW, written exclusions.
4. **Cash management.** Separate business account, deposits before work, *Profit First* by Mike Michalowicz for the operating rhythm.
5. **Case studies and referral asks.** After every delivery, in writing, within a week.

### AI skills, in order, each one producing a sellable asset

1. **Claude Projects and custom Skills** for each deliverable: partner-research skill, candidate-screening skill, quote-follow-up skill, review-reply skill. This is where delivery hours fall.
2. **Claude Cowork and connectors** (Google Sheets and Drive, HubSpot, QuickBooks). This is also the content of the Claude setup product.
3. **Vision and document handling**: job photos into remnant listings and social posts; resumes into scorecards; messy spreadsheets into clean CRM imports.
4. **Claude Code as your no-code builder**: have it build and host small tools for you, such as a CSV dedupe utility, a partner scorecard dashboard, a candidate pipeline tracker. You direct; it writes.
5. **Later, Agent SDK and MCP** for automated follow-up with human approval, once retainers justify the time.

### Tools budget (roughly $100–$250 per month to start)

Claude plan with Cowork; a Google Workspace domain; HubSpot free; one email-sending tool once outreach starts; a job-board budget per hire, billed through to the client; data enrichment pay-as-you-go.

## 7. Next 30 days

1. **Price Victory.** Write the two-option proposal in section 2. Deliver it by phone. Record the objection, whatever it is.
2. **Pick ten adjacent Austin companies** (cabinet shops, tile and flooring, remodelers, pool and outdoor living) and run your own Pipeline Build on them. Book five conversations.
3. **Build three Claude skills:** partner research, candidate screen, quote follow-up. Use them on items 1 and 2 so they are tested before a client sees them.
4. **Write the one-page price sheet** from section 4 and a one-page SOW template.
5. **Attend one meeting** of the local Home Builders Association, NARI, or NKBA chapter. Ask three owners which they would pay for first: more partner jobs or a filled role.
6. **Set up the business basics** if not done: LLC or equivalent, business bank account, invoicing.

## 8. Open questions

1. Are your ten hours mostly evenings and weekends? Recruiting screens fit that pattern well; partner calls fit it less well.
2. Would you enjoy recruiting again? It is the highest-fee, highest-pain option on the map, but only if you want the phone time.
3. Is a $100–$250 per month tools budget acceptable before the second paying client?
4. Does your employment agreement restrict outside work or client types? A one-time check, so Finchby's growth never collides with the day job.
