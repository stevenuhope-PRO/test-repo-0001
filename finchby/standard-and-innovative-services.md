# Finchby Paper 5: Standard and Innovative Services for Small and Medium Businesses, with a Six-Month Trial Ladder for Victory Countertops

*Working draft, 2026-10-04. Fifth paper in the series. Builds on Paper 4 (`evening-weekend-services.md`) and turns it into an operating plan.*

---

## 0. Where things stand

- **Hours:** 10 per week, mostly weekends, some evenings.
- **Victory Countertops:** an unpaid lab for about six more months. Try several services, measure which ones the owner values, convert what sticks to paid at month six.
- **Markets:** Austin remotely (Victory and adjacent trades), Louisville locally (where in-person networking happens).
- **Skills:** will learn a site builder, slowly. Bookkeeping is open but undecided.
- **Strategy:** standard services first to earn trust, automation built behind them so each one gets cheaper to produce. Compounding over linear.
- **New idea:** generative and agent optimization, a nice-to-have for Victory but a possible long-term product across industries.

## 1. The operating principle: standard in front, automation behind

Every offer lives in one of three layers. The buyer only ever sees the first two. The third is where the long-term value accumulates.

| Layer | What it is | Who it is for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Standard** | Services every small business already buys and understands in one sentence | Trust-building with new clients | Google Business Profile management, review replies, monthly newsletter, website care |
| **2. Enhanced** | The same standard service with an AI-era upgrade the incumbents do not offer | Differentiation at the same price | Profile management that also monitors whether the business appears in ChatGPT and Google AI answers |
| **3. Innovative** | New categories sold as audits and programs, and later as products | Door-openers now, cross-industry products later | AI Visibility Audit, agent-readiness checklist, automation builds |

Rule: nothing goes into Layer 3 as a standalone product until the same work has been done inside Layers 1 and 2 for at least ten clients.

## 3. The innovative layer

### 3A. AI Visibility: the generative and agent optimization idea, assessed

**The evidence that this matters now.** About 45% of consumers have asked an AI assistant to find a local business, and ChatGPT is now the third most common source of local recommendations after Google and Facebook. Yet only about 1.2% of business locations ever get recommended by ChatGPT. Google's AI Overviews and Gemini pull primarily from Google Business Profile data. ChatGPT's local data comes largely from Foursquare, with Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor, and the Better Business Bureau also feeding AI answers. The levers are unglamorous: a complete profile, consistent listings everywhere, LocalBusiness and FAQ structured data on the site, and reviews that mention the service, the city, and the outcome.

**What the market charges.** Agencies price small-business generative engine optimization retainers at roughly $1,500–$3,000 per month, with audits anywhere from about $700 at the low end to $8,000–$25,000 for enterprise-grade work. Monitoring tools cost $29–$50 per month. Almost none of this is being sold to a countertop shop or a Louisville restaurant, because the agencies are chasing mid-market budgets.

**The honest assessment.** For a local business, AI visibility is mostly excellent local SEO plus structured data plus listing hygiene, measured against AI answers instead of search rankings. Measurement is immature, results cannot be guaranteed, and the term is attracting hype. That is exactly why it works for Finchby as a door-opener rather than a standalone product: owners are curious, nobody local is offering it, and Claude can produce the audit in an hour or two.

**How to sell it, in three stages.**

| Stage | Offer | Price | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Now | **AI Visibility Audit**: run twenty realistic prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode; check profile completeness, listing consistency, schema, review language; deliver a two-page report with fixes | $300–$500, free for the first five Louisville prospects | Month 3 for Victory; door-opener from month 3 in Louisville |
| Within a year | **AI Visibility module** inside profile management: schema and FAQ content, listing consistency, monthly AI-mention monitoring | Add $100–$200 per month to the care plan | After ten audits |
| Long term | **AI Visibility and Agent-Readiness program** for multi-location and B2B companies, across industries | $500–$1,500 per month | After the module has run for ten clients |

**Agent-readiness, specifically.** Agentic commerce in 2026 is mostly about e-commerce catalogs and B2B procurement: machine-readable products, specifications, and terms that buying agents can compare. For a local service business, agent-ready means something simpler and sellable today: bookable online with a real scheduling link, a clear service list with price ranges, structured data for services and hours, consistent machine-readable listings, and a lead response that answers in seconds. Sell it as a checklist section inside the audit, not as a product of its own yet.

### 3B. AI-first lead response

AI receptionists now cost $25–$899 per month, with most small businesses paying $99–$299 for 24/7 answering, appointment booking, and CRM integration. Reseller and white-label tiers exist at roughly $99–$299 per month for an agency account. For home services, where a missed call is a lost job, this is the most immediately valuable AI product on the market. Finchby's role is implementation and management: configure the greeting, the qualifying questions, the booking calendar, and the hand-off, then review transcripts monthly. The same configuration template serves every client, so it compounds. Setup $300–$500, management $100–$200 per month on top of the tool.

### 3C. Automation and Claude workflow setup

Small-business automation retainers run $500–$1,500 per month, starter projects $1,000–$3,500. This is the compounding skill from Paper 4, and the plan is unchanged: build it for Finchby's own delivery first, then sell it to clients who already trust you from the standard services.

### 3D. The Claude Partner Network, assessed

Anthropic opened its partner program in March 2026 and launched the Claude Partner Network in June 2026 with three tracks (Consulting, Technology, and Services) and four certifications, including Claude Certified Associate and Claude Certified Developer. Tens of thousands of firms have applied and more than ten thousand consultants are certified. The network is aimed at enterprise buyers, so do not expect leads from it. The certifications are a different matter: the Associate certification is a credible learning milestone for the next six to twelve months and a line on the Finchby site that no local competitor will have.

### 3E. Vertical innovations worth keeping

Two ideas from the earlier papers remain innovative, cheap to deliver, and specific to the trades: the **remnant-to-cash program** for fabricators (photos in, Claude catalogs, listings out, small vanity jobs back) and the **partner scorecard** (which builders, designers, and realtors actually send work). Both are month-five and month-six material for Victory.

## 4. Victory Countertops: the six-month trial ladder

One service per month. Each has a one-page written scope, a logged hour count, a success signal, and an automation asset that must exist before the next month starts. A ten-minute feedback call with the owner at the end of each month is the practice ground for sales conversations without yet asking for money.

| Month | Service tried | What Victory gets | Asset Finchby builds | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Profile management, review replies, review-request flow | Complete profile, every review answered, requests sent after each install | Review-reply skill; post generator; request templates | Reviews per month rise; owner reads the replies |
| 2 | Partner newsletter to the 1,000-contact list | One newsletter to builders, designers, and realtors; segmented list; compliant footer | Newsletter skill; segment templates; send checklist | Opens, replies, at least one partner conversation |
| 3 | AI Visibility Audit and fixes | Report, then schema, FAQ content, and listing consistency on Google, Yelp, Foursquare, BBB, Nextdoor | Audit prompt pack; report template; fix checklist; schema generator | Victory appears in AI answers for Austin countertop prompts; owner asks questions |
| 4 | Quote follow-up cadence and after-hours lead response | Sequences for open quotes; AI receptionist configured for missed and after-hours calls | Follow-up sequence library; receptionist configuration template | Quotes closed from follow-up; calls captured that would have been missed |
| 5 | Remnant-to-cash listings and social from job photos | Remnant catalog with listings; three posts a week from install photos | Photo-to-listing skill; Canva templates | Remnant inquiries; small vanity jobs |
| 6 | Monthly business brief and partner scorecard | One page: jobs by source, top partners, reviews, pipeline | Report generator from CRM and accounting exports | Owner forwards the brief to someone |

**At month six, the paid conversation.** Propose a bundle of the two or three services that produced signals, at $500–$900 per month, with a stated start date. Ask for a written case study and a reference. Keep Victory exclusive among Austin fabricators, and say so; it is part of the value. If the answer is no, the six months still produced six assets, one case study, and a tested sales conversation.

Three rules for the lab: set the end date now and tell the owner; never add a seventh unpaid service; and log hours from day one so you know the real cost of each service before you price it.

## 5. Two markets, one menu

**Austin, remotely.** Every service in the menu is remote-deliverable. Victory's case study sells to adjacent trades: cabinet shops, tile and flooring, remodelers, pool and outdoor living. Sales happen by email and scheduled weekday-evening calls. Exclusivity applies only to Austin fabricators, which leaves every other trade open.

**Louisville, locally.** This is where evening networking happens, so this is where the many-small-clients version of the care plan grows. In 2026 Greater Louisville Inc. merged with the Louisville Economic Development Alliance and became One Louisville, which runs networking events and a member directory and houses small-business advising through the Kentucky Small Business Development Center and SCORE Louisville, with more than sixty volunteer counselors. The Louisville Independent Business Alliance represents exactly the independent restaurants, shops, and service businesses that buy care plans. The home-building and remodeling trades have their own local association. One event a month, and five free AI Visibility Audits as door-openers from month three, is the plan.

**Louisville fabricators are fair game.** The countertop playbook built for Victory can be sold to Louisville stone shops with no conflict at all.
