## 0. Updated constraints

- **Hours:** 10 per week, mostly weekends, some evenings.
- **Recruiting:** open to it, for business types other than stone fabrication, where skilled cutters come through word of mouth.
- **Employer:** outside work is permitted. One risk removed.
- Unchanged: no-code, building with Claude; enjoys B2B relationships; 30 years of phone, tech support, and recruiting work; the current client is unpaid; long-term target $90K–$120K.

### What weekend hours actually mean

Weekend availability is a real asset for some work and a real limit for other work. Being precise about which is which drives the whole selection below.

| Weekend hours are good for | Weekend hours are bad for |
|---|---|
| Delivery of anything asynchronous: research, writing, data cleanup, analysis, drafting | Selling to business owners, who are either off or on the floor |
| Phone screens with job candidates, who work weekdays and answer on Saturdays | Daytime-responsive services: ad monitoring, live lead response, IT support |
| Phone interviews with a client's consumer customers, who are home on weekends | Anything with weekday deadlines you cannot control, unless you build slack in |
| Building skills, templates, and Claude tooling | Services that require being on site during business hours |

Practical consequence: sales conversations happen on weekday evenings and lunch breaks, booked in advance. Delivery happens on weekends. Services that need weekday daytime presence are out.

## 1. What SMBs already pay for

Outsourcing surveys for 2026 show about 37% of small businesses outsource at least one function, up from 28% in 2021, and most plan to hold or increase that spend. The functions they outsource most:

| Function | Share of small businesses outsourcing it |
|---|---|
| IT services and support | 54% |
| Accounting and bookkeeping | 52% |
| HR and payroll processing | 45% |
| Digital marketing and content | 43% |
| Customer service and support | 32% |
| Legal | 28% |

These are the crowded categories. They are served by established firms, offshore providers, and software, with prices pushed down accordingly. A solo operator with 10 weekend hours does not win there.

The opportunity sits in the gaps between those categories: work that owners know they need, that no established vendor category owns, and that still gets done badly or not at all. Six such gaps show up repeatedly in the research: filling hard roles, following up on leads and partners, knowing why customers buy or leave, writing down how the business runs, winning public-sector bids, and overpaying vendors.

### What SMBs will and will not pay for in AI

The AI data matters because it tells you how to position Claude. About 58% of small businesses now use generative AI, up from 40% a year earlier, but only about 18% have paid for an AI tool. A third spend nothing per month, and only 10% spend more than $250. The leading barriers are data security and distrust of accuracy. AI consultants quoting $5K–$25K engagements are aiming at a buyer most SMBs are not.

**Conclusion: AI is your margin, not your product.** Sell the outcome (a filled role, a followed-up pipeline, a written operations manual, a submitted proposal). Use Claude to deliver it in a third of the hours. Keep a small Claude-setup offering as a learning lab and a door-opener, not as the business.

## 3. Three coherent portfolios

Any single service above can make money. A business needs a shape. The scored services group into three portfolios, each with one buyer, one method, and one story.

| Portfolio | Services | Core skill it monetizes | Weekend fit | Typical first-year unit economics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **People** | Flat-fee recruiting for hard roles; hiring system setup; onboarding packs; optional hourly pipeline | Your recruiting and phone background | High | $2,500–$4,500 per hire, 1–2 hires per month at 10 hours per week |
| **Customers** | Partner pipeline retainer; Customer Truth interviews; lead-source audit; reputation; reactivation | The Victory work plus the phone | Medium-high | 2–3 retainers at $1,000–$1,500 plus 1 study per quarter |
| **Operations and documents** | Operations manuals; CRM and Claude setup; public-sector proposals; vendor cost reduction; monthly brief | Interviewing, structuring, and writing with Claude | Very high | 1 manual or proposal per month at $2,500–$4,500 plus small retainers |

### 3A. Which to lead with

**Recommended primary: People.** The pain is the loudest across every SMB type, the fee per unit is the highest you can command today, candidate screens fit Saturdays and evenings exactly, agencies leave a wide price umbrella to sit under, and you have done the work before. It also transfers across industries, so you are never dependent on one vertical. It depends on one thing: whether you want the phone time with candidates. If that answer is lukewarm, lead with Customers instead.

**Recommended secondary: Customers.** It keeps the Victory story alive, cross-sells to the same owners (the person who needs an installer also needs more partner referrals), and the Customer Truth study is a product almost nobody offers at an SMB price.

**Year two expansion: Operations and documents.** Operations manuals are the most weekend-compatible service on the list and a natural follow-on once an owner trusts you. Public-sector proposal writing is the highest-ticket skill you can learn with Claude in a year, and it connects back to the trades, since contractors bid school, city, and county work.

### 3B. Why not a completely different category

Nothing in the research argues for leaving B2B services to owner-operated companies. The three portfolios all sell to the same buyer, all monetize phone, research, and structured writing, all run on Claude for margin, and all survive the weekend constraint. The decision is which door to walk through first, not which building.

## 4. Profitability path at 10 weekend hours

Gross revenue before tools and taxes. Assumes the People-primary, Customers-secondary mix. Hours include selling and admin.

| Stage | When | Mix | Monthly gross | Hours per week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First dollar | Months 0–3 | 1 paid hire or 1 retainer, possibly discounted; Victory priced | $500–$2,500 total | 10 |
| Proof | Months 3–12 | 1 hire per month, 1 retainer, 1 hiring-system setup per quarter | $3,000–$5,000 | 10 |
| Templated | Year 2 | 1–2 hires per month, 2 retainers, 1 study or manual per quarter, 2 bundle clients | $6,000–$9,000 | 10, the ceiling |
| Full-time trigger | When the templated stage holds for 3 months with 5+ warm conversations in hand | 3–4 hires per month, 5–6 retainers, 1–2 manuals or proposals per month | $14,000–$22,000 | 35–40 |

The full-time stage clears $90K–$120K net. The two numbers to track from month one are conversion (conversations to paid work) and effective hourly rate (how far Claude templating cuts delivery hours).

## 5. How to reach SMB owners without weekday hours

Owners buy from people they were introduced to. These referrers see dozens of SMBs each and are reachable by email and scheduled calls:

- **CPAs and bookkeepers.** They know who is hiring, who is disorganized, and who is selling.
- **Business brokers.** Every listing needs an operations manual and clean customer data.
- **Insurance and commercial-banking contacts.** They hear "I can't find people" daily.
- **Franchisors and franchise consultants.** Multi-unit franchisees hire constantly and follow systems.
- **Trade associations and chambers**, including the local Home Builders Association, NARI, NKBA, and restaurant association chapters. Evening meetings fit your hours.
- **The Small Business Development Center network.** Free advising to SMBs; advisors refer out specialized help.
- **Your own Pipeline Build**, run against your target list of ten referrers, then ten prospects.
