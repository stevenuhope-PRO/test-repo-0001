# Finchby Product Expansion Roadmap

*Working draft, 2026-10-04. Built from the Victory Countertops engagement as described, public information about that customer, and the current Claude product landscape. The Victory project folder was not reachable from this session, so items marked ⚑ should be checked against it.*

---

## 1. Starting point

What Finchby has already demonstrated at Victory Countertops (Austin, TX):

- Grew a B2B contact list from about 480 to over 1,000 contacts (list building and enrichment).
- Researched those contacts to rank likely profitability (account scoring).
- Flagged contacts likely to cost more than they return (risk screening).

Those are three distinct, sellable capabilities, and the customer profile is sharp. Victory is an established trade contractor: fabricating and installing stone since 2003, roughly 4.5 stars across about 100 public reviews, differentiated on a 5–7 business day install after templating and a 2-year labor warranty. A business like that grows through relationships with builders, remodelers, designers, and realtors far more than through consumer advertising. Its contact list *is* its pipeline.

Constraints that shape every recommendation below:

- Finchby is a side income today. Cash flow and low delivery hours matter more than scale.
- In 5–10 years it may be the primary income, so near-term products should build assets that compound: case studies, written playbooks, reusable Claude skills.
- Your edge is several months of hands-on Claude work. Most small contractors, and most local marketing freelancers, do not have that yet.

---

## 2. The strategic shape

Three moves, in priority order:

1. **Productize the Victory work into a named, fixed-price package and sell it to adjacent trades in Central Texas.** Cabinets, flooring, tile, plumbing, pool and outdoor living, remodelers, custom builders. They share the same builder/designer/realtor contact universe, so research gets cheaper with each client, and the Victory story lands immediately. Avoid selling it to Victory's direct competitors in Austin; exclusivity is your most valuable gift to your first reference customer.

2. **Follow the data with a recurring activation retainer.** A list is only worth money once it is worked. Outreach sequences, referral-partner nurturing, review generation, and a monthly brief turn a one-time project into monthly revenue. Recurring revenue is the only thing that makes the "primary income" version possible.

3. **Add Claude-native offerings as a second ladder.** Set up Claude for Small Business for owners, build vertical skill packs, run training. These have near-zero cost of goods, lean on your actual edge, and position you as the "AI for the trades" person in your market before that niche fills.

## 3. Offering catalog

Legend. **Readiness**: how much of the skill you already have (High / Medium / Low). **Claude leverage**: how much of the delivery Claude can carry.

### 3A. Sell now (built on proven skills)

| # | Offering | Buyer and problem | Readiness | Claude leverage | Pricing shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pipeline Build** (list expansion and enrichment) | Contractors whose referral network lives in a phone and a spreadsheet | High | High | One-time, tiered by list size |
| 2 | **Account Scoring Report** ("who is worth your time") | Owners who treat every contact alike | High | High | Add-on or standalone |
| 3 | **Risk Screen** (negative-value flags) | Owners burned by slow payers, defunct firms, bad-fit accounts | High | High | One-time, per list |
| 4 | **CRM Cleanup and Import** (dedupe, standardize, load into HubSpot/Jobber/etc.) | Businesses running on three half-used spreadsheets | Medium | High | One-time |
| 5 | **Market Map** (competitors, partners, suppliers in a metro or trade) | Owners expanding to a new area or service line | Medium–High | High | One-time |

**1. Pipeline Build.** Your flagship. Define it by inputs (their current list, their ideal customer profile, their service area), outputs (an enriched sheet with firmographics, role, best contact channel, source, and a confidence rating), and a delivery window. Sell tiers by list size, for example up to 500, 1,000, or 2,500 contacts. ⚑ Turn the exact method used for Victory into a written SOP and an internal Claude skill so delivery hours fall on every repeat.

**2. Account Scoring Report.** The "most profitable" analysis, generalized into a rubric: typical job size, frequency, speed to pay, referral reach, fit with the client's differentiators. Deliver a ranked list plus a one-page "top 25 to call this quarter." This is the page owners actually read, so it is the page that gets you referred.

**3. Risk Screen.** The negative-value flagging as its own product. Sources: public reviews, secretary-of-state registrations, public lien or court records where available, news, website status. Position it two ways: "don't waste a sales call" on new contacts, and a screen on the client's *existing* customer list. Low effort, high perceived value, and no local competitor is selling it.

**4. CRM Cleanup and Import.** The natural follow-on once a list exists. Requires learning one or two CRMs well. HubSpot's free tier is the obvious first; Jobber and Housecall Pro are common in home services. Claude for Small Business connects to HubSpot, which means you can deliver and hand off inside the same tool the owner will keep using.

**5. Market Map.** The same research muscle pointed at the market instead of the contact list. Useful when a client considers a second location, a new trade line (a fabricator adding cabinets), or a new partner channel.

### 3B. Add in the next 6–18 months (adjacent; some new skills)

| # | Offering | Buyer and problem | Readiness | Claude leverage | Pricing shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | **Pipeline Activation retainer** (outreach sequences, partner nurture, monthly brief) | Package 1 clients who will not work the list themselves | Medium | High | Monthly |
| 7 | **Referral Partner Program design** (builder/designer/realtor incentives, partner scorecards) | Trade contractors dependent on referrals | Medium | Medium | One-time plus optional monthly |
| 8 | **Reputation Ops** (review requests, drafted review responses, Google Business Profile upkeep) | Any local business that lives on reviews | Medium | High | Monthly |
| 9 | **Dormant Customer Reactivation** (mine past jobs for repeat and referral) | Businesses with years of job history and no follow-up | Medium–High | High | One-time, or share of recovered revenue |
| 10 | **Claude for Small Business setup and onboarding** (connect QuickBooks/HubSpot/Google, configure workflows, train the owner) | Owners curious about AI with no time to learn it | Medium–High | Medium | One-time plus optional coaching |
| 11 | **Vertical Skill Packs** (quote follow-up, estimate-to-invoice, install-day comms, remnant listings) | Trades with repetitive admin | Medium | High | Per pack, or bundled with #10 |
| 12 | **Claude for the Trades training** (association workshops, 1:1 coaching) | Association chapters, owners, office managers | Medium–High | Low | Per session or hourly |
| 13 | **Monthly Business Brief** (jobs by source, margin by partner, pipeline health from QuickBooks/CRM exports) | Owners flying blind between tax seasons | Medium | High | Monthly, usually bundled with #6 |
| 14 | **Inbound Lead Response** (draft replies to quote-form and missed-call leads within minutes; owner approves) | Contractors losing jobs to whoever answers first | Medium | High | Monthly |

**6. Pipeline Activation** is the most important product in this document. It converts a project client into a retainer client, and retainers are what a primary-income business is built on. Scope it tightly: a fixed number of sequences per month, a fixed number of partner touchpoints, one monthly brief, one 30-minute call. Email compliance (CAN-SPAM for email, documented consent before any texting under the TCPA) is part of the deliverable, and a selling point.

**10. Claude for Small Business setup** is a real opening. Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business in May 2026 inside Claude Cowork, with prebuilt agentic workflows, reusable skills, and connectors for QuickBooks, HubSpot, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Canva, DocuSign, PayPal, and Slack, included with the subscription. The product exists. What owners lack is someone to connect it to their actual tools, pick the three workflows that matter, and show the office manager how to approve outputs before anything sends or pays. That implementation gap is a service business, and it will not stay empty for long.

**11. Vertical Skill Packs** are where niche knowledge compounds. Examples for fabricators and adjacent trades: template-to-install scheduling messages, quote follow-up cadences, remnant and offcut listing posts (fabricators sit on sellable remnants), review requests with job photos after install, and "thank you for the referral" notes to partners. Build them once for Victory, then sell them to the next five clients.

**12. Training** has the best margin and the lowest delivery risk of anything here. The local Home Builders Association, NARI, and NKBA chapters are both a venue and a lead source for products 1–3.

**14. Inbound Lead Response** addresses the single biggest conversion lever for contractors: response time. Claude drafts the first reply and a qualifying question set; the owner or office manager approves and sends. Low build effort once a skill pack exists; high perceived value.

Hold #18 until you have five to ten Pipeline Build clients. Selling the service first tells you what the product should be.

## 5. Recommended package ladder

Three packages, each designed to sell the next.

| Package | Contents | Price range (assumption) | Target delivery hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Pipeline Build** | #1 + #2 + #3 for one list | $1,500–$4,500 one-time, by list size | 12–30 hours |
| **B. Pipeline Activation** | #6 + #13, optional #8 and #14 | $750–$2,000 per month | 4–8 hours per month |
| **C. Claude Setup and Coaching** | #10, optional #11 and #12 | $750–$2,000 setup; $300–$600 per month coaching | 6–12 hours setup; about 2 hours per month |

Price assumptions target owner-operated contractors roughly in the $1M–$10M revenue range in Central Texas. Contractor-focused fractional marketing firms publicly quote from about $3,500 per month for 20 hours, so these retainers sit well under the alternative while leaving you an effective rate above $100 per hour once delivery is templated. ⚑ Check these against what Victory actually paid and how many hours the work took.

## 6. Niche and positioning

- **Stay vertical first.** Home-improvement and construction trades in Central Texas. Shared contact universe, repeatable research, instant referenceability.
- **Protect Victory.** Do not sell Pipeline Build to competing Austin fabricators. Offer exclusivity per trade per metro as a premium. It is a differentiator and a reason for Victory to refer you.
- **Positioning line to test:** "Finchby turns a contractor's referral network into a worked pipeline, and sets up Claude so the office can keep it going."
- **Channels.** The Victory case study (quantified, with permission). Association chapter meetings and newsletters. Suppliers who serve many contractors at once: slab yards, cabinet distributors, tile showrooms. And your own Pipeline Build, run against *your* target list of adjacent trades.

### Legal and compliance (confirm with a Texas CPA and an attorney)

- Texas taxes data processing and information services, with a partial exemption. List research and enrichment may fall under these categories. Confirm how to invoice and remit sales tax.
- CAN-SPAM applies to B2B email. The TCPA requires consent before texting or autodialed calls.
- Add a short data-handling clause to the SOW: what you collect, how long you keep it, and that the client owns the deliverable.
- Errors and omissions insurance once retainers start.

### Skills to learn, ranked by return on time

1. **Packaging and selling a fixed-scope offer.** Discovery call script, one-page proposal, objection handling. Everything else depends on this.
2. **One CRM, deeply** (HubSpot first), including imports and basic automation.
3. **Email deliverability basics** (domain setup, warm-up, list hygiene) before selling #6.
4. **Claude Cowork and skill authoring**, so every delivery becomes a reusable skill. This is also the content of Package C.
5. **Claude Code for small scripts and dashboards** (CSV cleanup, dedupe, simple reports). Opens #13 and #15.
6. **Google Business Profile and local SEO fundamentals** for #8.
7. **Claude Agent SDK and MCP** for #16, once retainers justify automation.

### Assets to build in the next 90 days

- Victory case study: before and after counts, what the scoring surfaced, one quote from the owner.
- A one-page Finchby site with the three packages and a booking link.
- SOP plus an internal Claude skill for Pipeline Build, so the second delivery takes half the hours.
- Intake questionnaire: ideal customer profile, service area, current list, CRM, goals.
- Proposal and SOW template, plus a pricing sheet.

### Time budget

At roughly 10 hours per week, a side business can hold about two active retainers plus one project. A suggested weekly split: 4 hours delivery, 3 hours sales and follow-up, 2 hours learning and asset-building, 1 hour admin. Guard the learning and asset time. It is what makes the 5–10 year version possible.

## 8. Risks and guardrails

- **Customer concentration.** One client is a hobby. Make landing clients two and three the only Q4 goal.
- **Data legality and quality.** Use sources whose terms allow the use. Keep provenance per contact. Review scoring yourself before it reaches the client.
- **Scope creep.** Fixed deliverables, written exclusions, change orders.
- **Trust in AI output.** Anything that touches a client's customers (emails, review replies, texts) gets human approval before it sends. Make that part of the pitch.
- **Building the product too early.** Service first, product later (#18).
- **Exclusivity conflicts.** Decide the policy before client two asks.

## 10. Questions that would sharpen this

The recommendations above stand under the stated assumptions. Answers here would change the ranking.

1. **Time.** How many hours per week can Finchby get now? Is that stable or seasonal?
2. **Income target.** What monthly net covers the loan payments, and what number would make going full-time worth it?
3. **Skills inventory.** What is your day-job background (sales, operations, finance, technical, trades)? Do you want to write code, or stay no-code with Cowork and skills?
4. **Victory economics.** ⚑ What did you charge, how many hours did it take, and which tools and data sources did you use? This also tells me what the SOP should contain.
5. **What the contacts are.** Mostly builders, remodelers, and designers, or homeowners? Did the profitability scoring use Victory's job history, or outside research only?
6. **Appetite for recurring work.** Are you comfortable owning a client's outreach month to month, or do you prefer discrete projects?
7. **Exclusivity.** Will you keep Victory exclusive and sell only to adjacent trades, or sell to other Austin fabricators too?
8. **Interests.** Which catalog items would you *enjoy* doing weekly? The ranking should bend toward those.
9. **Geography.** Central Texas only, or remote clients anywhere?
10. **Risk tolerance.** Willing to spend on data tools and a sending platform before the second client, or bootstrap entirely?
