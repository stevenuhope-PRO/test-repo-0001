Method

Our approach

We establish what the AI engines say about a business today, correct the sources they read, improve the website, and measure the result every month. The order matters: advertising a business whose facts disagree only sends buyers to a contradiction.

First

Establish the facts

We put real buyers' questions to each engine, several times, and record the answers: whether the firm is named, what is said, which sources are cited and which competitors appear. That record is the baseline for everything after.

Second

Agree one identity

A short meeting with the owner settles the facts: the true name, address, telephone, hours, founding year, credentials and terms. These become the master record.

Third

Correct the sources

Core profiles are claimed and completed; the directories and list sites the engines cite are corrected or joined. Every entry is checked against the master record.

Fourth

Improve the website

Clutter is removed, pages are labelled for machines, and the missing pages are written, each answering its question in the first two sentences and stating real prices, lead times and dates.

Fifth

Build the reputation

A routine for asking each customer for a review, and for answering every one. Where an engine repeats an unfair claim, the true position is published and the claim answered at its source.

Sixth

Measure, monthly

The same questions are put again and compared with the baseline, alongside the reports from Google, Bing and the firm's own enquiry form. The plan is adjusted from the figures.

A point of difference

What we advise against

Every Finchby plan carries a list of things not to spend time or money on. A consultant's advice is worth more when it includes what to leave alone.

The list changes as the engines change, and we revise it with evidence. At present it commonly includes the items opposite.

  • Paid “best of” badges and placements on sites that merely copy their data from Google and Yelp.
  • Buying, filtering or rewarding reviews. It risks suspension of the profile and is not honest.
  • Fashionable technical files that no major engine presently reads.
  • Daily tracking subscriptions before a monthly check has shown a gap worth watching.
  • Advertising before the foundations agree.
  • Text hidden in tabs, scripts or PDFs, where AI crawlers cannot read it.

Questions commonly asked

Plain answers

Can you guarantee that ChatGPT will recommend my business?

No, and no one honestly can. The same question returns the same businesses only some of the time. What can be done is to raise the share of answers in which you appear and to see that the facts given are correct. We measure both.

How does this differ from ordinary search engine optimization?

It includes it, but the weight falls elsewhere. AI engines draw heavily on third-party sources: maps data, reviews, directories and list sites. Much of the work therefore lies off your own website.

How long before there is a result?

Corrected listings and profiles can be read by the engines within weeks. New website pages and a body of reviews take longer. We plan in ninety-day terms and check monthly.

Do you need to rebuild my website?

Seldom. Most sites need cleaning, labelling and a handful of new pages. We work in WordPress with Elementor and Yoast SEO, and alongside your existing developer where there is one.

Who does the work?

The principal, assisted by AI research tools that he directs and checks. Some tasks, such as collecting facts, photographs and reviews, necessarily fall to the owner; the plan says which.

What kinds of business do you take on?

Established local firms whose customers ask “who should I call?”: trades, fabricators, contractors, showrooms and professional practices.
