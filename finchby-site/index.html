The matter in brief

Buyers now ask an assistant

People put the question to ChatGPT or Google's AI and receive three or four names. A firm that is not among them is not considered.

The engines read other people's pages

Their answers are assembled from directories, list sites, maps and reviews, more than from your own website. Whatever those sources say, right or wrong, is repeated.

Most of the remedy is orderly work

One consistent set of facts, complete profiles, a website that states its prices and terms plainly, and a steady flow of honest reviews.

From a current engagement

A stone fabricator in Central Texas

An established family-run fabricator with strong reviews and long-standing trade credentials was nonetheless absent from nearly every AI answer to the question “who are the best in the city?”

Our assessment found the cause was not the quality of the firm. Two websites and two telephone numbers were in circulation, three different founding years were quoted, and the list sites the engines relied upon carried wrong details. No page stated the firm's prices, terms or credentials.

The engagement set these right in order: one master record, the core profiles, the cited list sites, the website, and a monthly check against the original baseline. The work continues.

How we conduct ourselves

  • Evidence before opinion. Every engagement begins with a recorded baseline, and is judged against it.
  • No promised rankings. Nobody controls what an AI engine says. We improve the odds and show the figures.
  • We say what not to buy. Each plan lists the tools, badges and placements we advise against.
  • Your accounts remain yours. Profiles are held in the company's name, never in ours.
  • Plain language. Plans are written to be read and acted upon by an owner, not an engineer.
The engines repeat whatever they read. Our work is to see that what they read about your firm is true, consistent and easy to find.
